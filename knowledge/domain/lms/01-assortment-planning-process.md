Introduction to Assortment Planning

## What is Retail Assortment Planning and Why is it Important?
First, let's start with the definition of retail itself. Retail is the selling of products to customers for a profit. The better a retailer is at identifying products that customers want to buy, the more likely the retailer is to be profitable. The objective of a retailer is to sell products consistently; if a retailer offers products that customers do not want, the business will quickly struggle.
At its core, retail revolves around serving customers who are willing to buy. Retail is often described as a blend of art and science; there are both qualitative and quantitative aspects that retailers must consider. Both contribute to the success of the retailer and the delight of the customer. Below are key factors retailers must manage in order to succeed:

### Five Success Factors for Retailers:
Customer Insight: Understanding the customer and selecting products that you believe the customer will value and want to buy.
Buying Acumen: Knowing how much of each product to buy and in which sizes.
Profitability: Knowing the right cost to buy at and the right price to sell at in order to make a profit while still appealing to customers.
Marketing Effectiveness: Drawing existing, new, and lapsed customers to the business; maintaining consistent brand image and messaging that resonates with the customer's values; and ensuring pricing and promotions attract sales without eroding profit.
Customer Experience: Creating a store or site experience that is pleasant and that maximizes selling opportunities through the right mix of products in the assortment.

## Assortment Planning is Critical to a Retailer's Success
Assortment Planning is the process that enables retailers to act on all of these dynamic factors. It is classically defined as the process of determining what products to carry, where and when to carry them, and how much to buy. Assortment Planning coordinates decisions related to products, marketing, profitability, and the mix offered across stores and digital channels.

## Objectives of Assortment Planning
The overarching objective of Assortment Planning is to make the right decisions about what products to select, how many to carry, how much to buy, how long products should be offered, and at which locations and price points they should be sold.
Determine what products are / are not working
Determine product strategy - how many products to carry next year, both in total and by category or attribute.
Determine location strategy - how many products stores can carry, based on factors such as sales volume.
Select products that fit the strategy.
Determine product attributes, price, and cost
Determine when products should flow into and out of the assortment (lifecycle planning).
Determine how much you expect to sell and, therefore, how much to buy and in which sizes.
Execute the actual buys by placing purchase orders with vendors / factories
Evaluate product performance after products arrive, to see if there are adjustments to be made based on actual sales results
Assortment Planning - What is "Planned"
Now that we understand why Assortment Planning is important, let us clarify what exactly is "planned."
There are two key concepts covered in this section:
Pre Season vs In Season Planning
Planning vs Forecasting

## Pre Season vs In Season Planning
The term "assortment planning" implies a strategy or roadmap for what we want to achieve. In Assortment Planning, users create strategies and future product visions. But what happens when products actually arrive and customers begin buying? A strong retailer both plans assortments in advance (before actual selling begins) and reacts once selling starts, because products may or may not perform as expected.
Assortment Planning covers both the "planning" and the "reacting" phases. In the industry, these are typically defined as follows:
Pre-Season: "Planning" and all related strategic and tactical activities before an item is received by the retailer (that is, before the cartons arrive at the retailer's warehouse).
In-Season: "Reacting" and revising the initial strategy based on actual selling information after the product has been received and distributed from the warehouse to stores, websites, or other selling channels.

## 3.1 Product Selection
This section reviews the detailed process steps for 3.1 Product Selection.
For design-led retailers, once the Design team creates the options for the line, Merchants can begin selecting the products that will be planned as part of the future assortment. For retailers who buy from a vendor line, the vendor or brand presents the line instead.

## 3.1.1 Design (or Vendor) Presents the Line
For design-led teams, once the Design team finalizes the internal design process, it presents the proposed options to Merchants. Generally, the Design team follows a design-to-adopt ratio, which means there will usually be more design options available than were requested in the Choice Count Plan.
At the time the line is presented, there may be a system "publish" event within the PLM system to reveal the line to partner systems. If a publish event or flag is part of the process, S5 can use it as a trigger to send design candidates and their data into S5 Assortment.
For retailers who buy from a vendor line, Buyers review products from the vendor catalog or line sheet. The vendor line sheet is uploaded into the retailer's master data management system through an item-creation process that assigns each vendor choice to the retailer's standard hierarchy and attributes.

## 3.1.2 Merchant Selects Products (Line Adopt)
Once the line is presented, merchants begin selecting which products they will adopt to fill the "choice count plan."
There are essentially two options for how Design/Vendor items can be adopted and made available for planning in S5 Assortment:
Option 1: If placeholders are part of the process in S5 Assortment, users leverage the "Design/Vendor Replace" functionality. Users can access placeholders they have already created and then select the Design (or Vendor) option that will replace each one. During this process, many attributes, along with the image, are copied from the design system and saved to the placeholder. At the time of replacement, the placeholder status also changes to real.
Option 2: If placeholder creation is not part of the process in S5, users can follow a simpler workflow in which they are shown a view of design options and can select the items they want to plan. Users can then specify attributes and parameters for the design item, ultimately generating the sales and inventory plan.
Regardless of how the design options are adopted, the final outcome is that the S5 Assortment Build module contains all of the "real" design options for which plans will be created.
During the line-adopt process, merchants should match Design/Vendor options to their original targets. They should also decide what role each actual design item will play in the future assortment, ensure the choices address the needs of a diverse customer base, and review or edit attribute values to maintain accurate master-data coding.

## 4.1 Quantification - Generate Sales & Receipt Plan
Once product selection has been completed, users can refine the parameter values used to generate the sales and inventory plans. The following steps describe the tactical adjustments Merchants can make to refine the system plan.

## 4.1.1 Assign Pre-Season Sales Rating
Each item in the plan will be assigned a "Pre-Season Sales Rating" value.
Pre-Season Sales Ratings provide the system with a qualitative assessment of expected performance and are used as an input when generating the recommended sales and inventory plan.
The Pre-Season Sales Rating is assigned on a 1-5 scale, with 5 being the strongest rating. If placeholders are used, the pre-season rating may be copied from a like item as a guide to how similar items have performed historically. Users are expected to review the rating for each item based on how they believe the product will perform. The system then uses this rating to estimate sales velocity based on expected item performance.

## 4.1.2 Assign Lifecycle Parameters
Users can also review and edit lifecycle parameters for products in the assortment. Lifecycle parameters are milestone events in an item's life that affect the sales and inventory plans in S5.
The lifecycle milestones that users will specify for each style color are:
Initial Receipt: The week in which a style-color should first be received into the Distribution Center (DC).
Debut Week: The week in which a style-color should begin selling.
Markdown Week: The week in which a style-color should move to markdown (MD), meaning a permanent price reduction and entry into liquidation mode.
Exit Week: The week in which a style-color should be fully exited from the assortment and no longer planned.

## 4.1.3 Input Price & Promotions Plans
Users review and edit the price, cost, and promotional strategies planned for products in the assortment. The price at which an item is offered, along with planned discounts, is used by the system to generate the sales and inventory plan. Changes to an item's average unit retail (AUR) directly affect the sales and inventory plans. Typically, weeks with larger discounts are planned to generate a lift in unit sales as customers respond to the promotion. The more accurate the planned discounts and AUR values are, the more accurate the resulting sales and inventory plans will be.
The following inputs can be made for price and promotions:
Ticket Price: Ticket Price (MSRP)
Discount Rates: A general average percentage off that is always applied (for example, to account for employee discounts).
Percentage-off Promotions: A planned promotion that offers an item at a given percentage off during a specific week.
Price-point Promotions: A planned promotion that offers an item at a fixed price for a given week.

## 4.1.4 Specify Order Mins, Presentation Mins (if needed)
Users can also specify constraints for inventory planning, such as order minimums, presentation minimums, and the number of weeks for which the presentation minimum should be respected by the system.

## 4.1.5 Specify Valid Sizes (if needed)
If needed, users can specify whether any sizes in the size range should not be planned. This functionality can support strategic size planning in cases where "fringe" sizes should or should not be offered.

## 4.1.6 Generate Sales and Receipt Plan by StyleColorSize
Once the required system attributes and parameter values are assigned, users execute the planning process. During the planning routine, the system generates predicted sales, inventory, receipts, and margin plans for each style-color by size, week, and location.

## 4.1.7 Validate / Adjust Sales Plan & APS
Once the plan has been generated, users can validate the resulting sales plan and APS for products in the assortment.
If the user believes the resulting sales plan is too high or too low, there are several ways to adjust it.
Option 1: Adjust the parameters that affect the plan (recommended).
Adjust the Pre-Season Sales Rating.
Adjust other planning parameters, such as the pricing or promotion plan.
Adjust the ranging plan.
Adjust valid sizes.
Option 2: Adjust the sales plan at the week level.
Users can override the sales plan value in any planned week by entering a sales quantity by style-color and week.
Option 3: Enter an APS adjustment to adjust all planned weeks proportionately.
If the plan needs to be reduced across all planned weeks, users can enter an "APS Adjustment." An APS Adjustment changes the sales plan proportionately for all future planned weeks.

## 4.1.8 Validate Timing & Frequency of Receipts
In addition to validating the sales plan, users can also validate and edit the receipt plan generated by the system.
Users can access views that display the receipt plan by style-color, size, and week. In these views, they can make the following edits:
Change the total receipt quantity for the week: the system will spread the quantity down to the size level.
Change the receipt quantity by size for a week: the system will aggregate the size quantities and calculate the total quantity for that week.
Lock a receipt week: this prevents changes to receipts for the locked week during re-planning.
Specify valid receipt weeks: users can identify which weeks should allow inbound vendor receipts and which should not.
The system also gives users the ability to view the system-recommended quantities, regardless of edits made, so they can identify any discrepancy between the edited plan and the system suggestion in a given week.

## 4.2 Reconciliation
After reviewing and editing the parameters assigned to individual products and generating sales plans, users can review the Assortment Plan roll-up at multiple levels of the product and location hierarchy and across attributes.
The Reconciliation activity ensures that the assortment plan is aligned with Assortment Strategies (Choice Plans) and Merchandise Financial Plans (MFP). Reconciliation also provides comparisons of planned performance over time, including comparisons to LY and LLY.

## 4.2.1 Reconcile to Assortment Choice Count Plan (CC Count Strategy)
Users review the Assortment roll-up against their original Choice Plan strategies. Reconciliation against the Choice Plan is not intended to be a strict or hard reconciliation. When reconciling the assortment against the Choice Count Plans, users should review the following:
Comparison of assortment receipts by floorset versus Choice Count Plan receipts by floorset.
Receipts and choice counts by floorset for new versus carryover products.
Receipts and choice counts by floorset by volume tier.
APS targets by floorset.
If the assortment roll-up differs from the Assortment Strategy Choice Count Plans, users must decide what action is appropriate, identify the products affected, and make the required edits to item parameters or weekly sales and inventory plan values.

## 4.2.2 Reconcile to Merchandise Financial Plans (Category x Week x Channel)
Users can also review the Assortment roll-up against Merchandise Financial Plans at the category, week, and channel level. In general, reconciliation to MFP product plans may not be a hard reconciliation for every metric.
If edits are needed because the Assortment Plan is misaligned with the MFP plans, users can modify item parameters and plan values. In some cases, teams may instead determine that the MFP plan itself should be updated.

## 4.2.3 Reconcile to Location Plans (if applicable)
If retailers are creating location plans (possibly as part of MFP), reconciliation to Location Plans can also be performed where applicable. Users compare the Location Plans to the roll-up of the Assortment Plans. In general, this reconciliation is not expected to be hard.

## 4.2.4 Specify Order Mins, Presentation Mins (if needed)
Users can also specify constraints for inventory planning, such as order minimums, presentation minimums, and the number of weeks for which the presentation minimum should be respected by the system.

## 4.2.5 Compare Roll-Up vs. History (LY, LLY)
As an additional validation point, users can compare the assortment roll-up to historical performance across the product and location hierarchy and across attributes. S5 Assortment provides flexible and easy ways for users to drill into intersections across product, time, and location in order to gain insights into the plan and identify areas of opportunity and risk.
If edits need to be made to the Assortment Plan, users can adjust item parameters and plan values within the assortment.

## 4.3 Approve & Publish
The final stage of the reconciliation process is the final review and approval stage. The objective of this activity is to review the assortment with cross-functional teams and leadership, socialize the plan, gather feedback, and make edits as needed. Once the plan is reviewed and approved, users can begin execution and issue purchase orders.

## 4.3.1 Final Review & Approve Process
Generally, before approval, Merchant and Planning teams present their assortment plan to Planning and Merchandising Leadership in a milestone meeting (often called a "Final Buy Meeting," "Investment Review Meeting," or something similar). During this approval, leadership reviews the assortment for product mix, reconciliation to Choice Count Plans, MFP Plans, and Location Plans, plans by Location Clusters, and variances to history for key attributes.
The final review process may be iterative and may require merchants to make multiple revisions before receiving final approval.

## 4.3.2 Final Approval to Publish
Once all assortment feedback has been received and edits have been made, the assortment is ready for final approval. After final approval is granted, users can begin the order-execution process.

## 4.3.3 Publish Buy Quantities
After final approval, users access S5 Assortment and "publish" buy quantities for the style-colors in the assortment for the weeks in the floorset. The act of publishing triggers the Merchant Buy Quantities to flow to the partner PO Management system for order creation.

## 5.1 Assortment Revision
Below is a process diagram of a standard assortment planning process, with 5.1 Assortment Revision Level 3 Steps highlighted in red outline below.

## 5.1 Revision
Once purchase orders have been issued, it is likely that the assortment plan will need to be revised, and the resulting "buy quantities" that were previously published may also be revised.

## 5.1.1 Post Publish Revisions
The S5 Assortment solution is designed to support realistic assortment scenarios, including assortment revisions and buy-quantity revisions. Revisions to the assortment can happen for many reasons, including:
Revisions to the plan due to changes in planned promotions.
Revisions to the plan in response to current business trends.
Revisions to the plan due to production issues (shortages, etc.).
Revisions to the plan due to the "fast track" addition of new products.
Revisions to the plan due to changes in Merchandise Financial Plans or Location Plans (for example, new stores being added).
If revisions are needed, users can follow the same item-editing processes described in the Buying and Reconciliation phases.
