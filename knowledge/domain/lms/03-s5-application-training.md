## Session Filters
Session filters are extremely powerful in enabling users to explore data in a “self-service” paradigm.  Similar to the filters you may use when shopping online, filters allow
users to pare down the data set based on product and location attributes and hierarchy.
Users can apply filters based on product or location attributes to refine the data set.  A  “Filter” button is available in each view. Filters are accessible in most
views, and are applied to most views in the user’s session, unless otherwise noted in the view.  If filters are not applicable in a view, a comment will be displayed in
the view.
Users can quickly identify if a session filter is ‘active’ or not.  The filter icon will turn green once session filters are applied.  Users can hover on the icon to
view which session filters are active.
Members displayed in session filters are “dynamic” in nature.  The list of valid “select-able” members that is displayed is generated based on the data set (scope,
department, etc).
Applying FIlters
1. Select the Filter Icon.  (if filters are applied, the filter icon will be green)
2. In the Session Filter panel, select either the Product or Location Tab.
3. Based on the tab selected, the system will display attributes.  Click in an accordion to display attribute values to show or hide
4. Search or select all attribute values within each section if needed
5. Clear All selections using the Clear button
6. Use the Submit button to submit selections and apply the criteria to the views.  Filter selections will apply across views.
Filter Quick View
When Session Filters have been applied, the system will highlight the "Filter" icon in Green. Hover over the Filter Icon to display a summary of the Filter Values that are actively being applied.

## Flow Status Toggles
Flow status is a time phased status that describes the ‘newness’ associated with a product at a given point in time.   Flow status ‘toggles’ are available in almost every workflow in the application. Users can easily filter results based on the assigned flow status.
System displays the following Flow Statuses:
•    New
•    Flow (Carryover)
•    Sell Down (Living on the Floor)

## Flow Status Definitions
Typical definitions for Flow Status are as follows:
Flow Status:  NEW
An item’s “New” period is typically defined as the period from its first inventory week (first week where BOH > 0), plus the next 4 weeks.
Flow Status: FLOW (or CARRYOVER):
An item’s “Flow” or “Carryover” phase is defined as the period between its NEW, and LIVING ON THE FLOOR (SELL DOWN) period.
Flow Status:  SELL DOWN (or LIVING ON THE FLOOR):
An item’s “Living on the Floor” or “Sell Down” period can be defined in multiple ways.  This status us used to define the period where the product stopped being funded orreplenished.
A typical way to define the start of the Sell Down period is below:
Using the items (Last On Order Week or Receipt Week) + [4]

## 1.1 Collection View
This view allows merchants to focus on the visual assortment, and find visual trends of what is, and what is not resonating with the customer.  The collection view is a customer centric view of the assortment.  Users can assess assortment mix, top/bottom sellers, top/bottom volume drivers by attribute, locations, etc.
Users can also quintile results to view what products are driving performance in sales bands, from the Top 20% down to the Bottom 20%.  In the context of assortment planning, this will let users know what items / volume they need to “protect” for next year, as well as which items they may not want to repeat.
Users can sort, group, and filter on images to draw conclusions about the assortment offering, merchandise mix, and performance on one view.  Items are “Fit” into one view, no scrolling for a true whitespace visual of the line.
Note:  The collection view is a "whiteboard" view, and does not allow for scrolling.  As such, this view has a limit of 300 products that can fit in the view.  If the threshold is exceeded, users will receive a warning message.
Features and Functionality
1. Access the view via the Left Nav menu.
2. The Collection view offers the ability to filter by Flow Status, and/or use Local Search to pare down results based on keywords
3. The Collection view also offers Sorting and Grouping functionality
4. Session filters can also be applied to further refine results.
Using Group By to Validate Merchandise Mix
Using the Group By feature allows users to review the assortment mix by attribute.  Users can validate how many products were offered across different location and product attributes, and summaries of key metrics, such as Sales Volume.
1.  Make a selection in the Group By dropdown
2.  Items in the view will be grouped into columns based on the attribute value selected.  Images within each column will be sorted by the Sort By metric.  Review Item counts,  Sales, Margin, Stk/Sls metrics.   Review images to view if there were any "holes" in the assortment.
Performing Pareto Analysis (Quintile Analysis)
The Group By feature also provides a value for "Performance" or "Sales Quintiles".   Grouping by this value will display items grouped by 20% sales bands.  In effect, users can see what items are driving the top 20%, as these are key volume drivers they need to "comp" for next year.
1.  In the Group By dropdown, Select "Performance" (or "Sales Quintiles").  The system will group items based on 5 equal performance bands based on Sales $.
In the example above, you can see only 1 item contributed to the Top 20% of sales, where the Bottom 20% of sales contained 58 products.  This means, for next year's assortment, the Merchant needs to find another product that is a large volume driver to "comp" the volume of last year's item. Alternatively, the Merchant may decide to de-risk next year and plan for more top sellers.
Drilling into Style Details via Style Pane
Users can click on images to invoke the Style Pane, which will provide detailed information about an item's attributes, price & cost, color options offered, Lifecycle, store range, and Life to Date (LTD) metrics.

Quintile Color Banding & Ratings Metrics
Color Banding
Color Banding is a feature in History Grid that help users quickly view exceptions and/or relative performance evaluations.   For select metrics, color banding represents that metric's evaluation based on the quintile it lies in, compared to its peers in the data set.  If the History Grid has subtotals applied, Color banding is performed within each Subtotal Group.
Quintile Based Color Bands have the following definitions:
a.    Dark Green = Top 20%
b.    Light Green = Above average (Top 20-40%)
c.    Tan = Average (Middle 40-60%)
d.    Orange= Below average (Bottom 20-40%)
e.    Red = Bottom 20%
Ratings
Ratings Metrics assign a value from 1 to 5 for a metric, based on quintile analysis, with a rating of 5 being the highest and 1 the lowest.
1. This example shows Quintile based Color Bands.  Within a subtotal group, the system will quintile results and display the corresponding color code, indicating which quintile the result falls into.
2. Ratings can also be configured.  For a selected metric, the system can display it's rating value (Quintile 1 to 5), 5 being "Best" or "Top 20%").    Ratings help simplify the analysis process and provide users with quick evaluations for product performance.
The "Star" or composite rating value can also be configured and displayed in this view.

## Clustering in S5
Clustering is the process of creating volume-based store groups for a period of time, for a group of products (ie, for a Department or Class, or even Vendor).  Clustering enables users to segment stores based on each store's relative performance for the data set (ie Sales by Month by [Department].    These clusters then allow users to assort products to store groups based on volume.
Clusters generated in the Clustering process are leveraged throughout the S5 Platform:  In Assortment Strategy, in Assortment Build / Assortment Planning and Ranging, in Analysis, and in Allocation.
Due to the wide reaching nature of clusters being leveraged in multiple areas  across the platform, the Clustering workflow includes Approval step.  This Approval requires that users take an additional controlled step to Approve Clusters that are generated, before they are used across the platform.  This allows more controlled, intentional changes to Clusters, prevents user error, and promotes streamlined, best practice processes in the organization.
A visual example of how stores are clustered based on Sales $ Volume, called Store Volume Grades.  Store Volume Grades are groups of stores that perform similarly for the Sales $ metric.
Note in the above illustration that ECOM (WEB) is its own grade, and stores are grouped highest to lowest based on Full Price Gross Sales $ (Stores are coded with values of "AA" to "E" in descending order)
As a best practice, retailers should have around ~5 Grade values.  Too few does not give enough differentiation;  Too many make the differences not material enough, and create more work to manage different assortments.

## How Does Clustering Benefit Retailers?
Clustering helps retailers vary their product assortment, and thus their buys, based on how stores are predicted to sell.  Since it is inefficient to assort unique assortments to every individual stores, Store Clusters (or Volume Grades) help group stores together based on similar performance, for ease in executing more complex strategies.
Here is an example of where Clustering Stores by Department, by [Quarter], can help teams adjust their assortments by store:
For the "Swimwear" department
Assume there is a store that is in a warm Climate [Store #1234], which receives product all year round
This store is in Florida, in a resort community whose residents live part of the year in the Northeast (Summer), and part of the year in Florida (winter)
As such, this store sees dramatic shifts in volume in the Summer Months vs the Winter Months.
In the Winter Months, this store is the number 1 volume driver for the company in Swimwear due to the increased traffic.  In the Summer Months, this store is a bottom performing store in swimwear, due to reduced traffic.
If we assorted the store the same amount of product for this department all year, the store would be over-inventoried and over-assorted in the Summer months.
Additionally, this store also sells products in other categories (like Sweaters or outerwear jackets), where it may be a bottom performing store all year round, due to the low appetite for products of those type in that region or climate.
In Summary - Assorting product using Store Volume Grades (Clusters) by [Category], by Month or Floorset by location, can make a material difference in the product offering, margins, and inventory turnover for a retailer.
As a note, typically clusters are created for a longer period of time, such as a Quarter or design season (~approx. 3 months for example), vs weekly or monthly.

3.1  Assortment Strategy - Concepts

## Assortment Strategy in S5
Assortment Strategy is the process of determining Assortment Framework and specifically outlines the Assortment targets in terms of new and carryover products to be offered in the future assortment period along with target productivity metrics like FP APS, FP Sell-Thru etc, all while staying within the boundaries of the product hierarchy, financial plan, and production/design constraints.
Purpose and Benefits of Assortment Strategy
Assortment strategy is important for creating the blueprint for the future assortment.
At a macro level, Assortment strategy workflow helps define the Assortment width and depth plan, and provides the foundation for Assortment Planning.
Additionally, Assortment Strategy direction can be used to influence the Assortment Planning models, to inform the system of users intended plans for the future (vs, just using LY history for predictions, which can be sub-optimal in a fashion apparel business model).
Assortment Strategy allows users to determine the following:
1.    Create targets for # of new CCs that are needed for a time period
         a.   Where, New CC targets represent the "MAX" number of CCs that are needed from design
2.    What is the composition of choices offered to the customer, across New vs Carryover
3.    What are the Target Productivity metrics (e.g. APS, FP Sell-Thru%) for New choices
4.    Ensure Balance with the Receipt Targets from MFP to ensure strategy is within financial boundaries

4.    What are the corresponding LY period;s definitions so the system can pull reference history data?
5.    What values will be assigned to Floorset Attributes  (ie Debut Season Code) so users can see products associated with the floorsets?

## 3. Seeding From MFP
The system offers the capability to “prime” or populate the plan with values as a starting point.  This is known as “seeding”, and is where the system copies values from a source and populates those as a starting point for the users’ planned values.  Users can then make edits as needed.  Seeding allows for more efficiency in providing a starting point for users, vs users starting with an entirely blank plan and needing to fill out every metric on their own.
 Seeding overwhelmingly uses LY as the source of data, which is copied into the TY plan as the starting point.
The system does have another source for seeding, which is MFP.
As highlighted previously in the document, MFP can only be seeded for the Receipts Metric.
•    Only Receipt Units will be seeded for MFP
•    MFP receipts are planned as Vendor to DC Receipts.
•    Receipts for Targets at the Cluster level are “DC to Store Receipts”.
•    As such, MFP receipts are not apples to apples with Target Receipts.   To overcome this, the system will take MFP values at the "total" level and spread those down to the "rows" using LY FP Sales Unit penetration.
All other metrics will be seeded using LY.

## 4.  Concepts for APS Planning
“APS” (Average per store per week) is a key metric for Target Setting.  APS represents the average per store per week that a “choice” (CC, stylecolor) will sell.  This is a key productivity metric in understanding how many products are needed to generate the sales.
For APS Planning, system will seed the following measures from LY:
-    Sales U
-    APS U
-    Uniformity % (also referred to as Distortion or Dilution %)
In this section, the following concepts for APS planning will be covered:
1.    APS Calculations at Aggregate Levels
2.    Proposal for APS Planning for Target Setting
3.    Max vs Average CCs at Aggregate Levels
4.    Making Changes to CC Counts at Aggregate Levels

### 4.1.   APS Calculations at Aggregate Levels
APS should be computed as [Sum of Sales ] / [Count of valid (Store x Week) ]combinations.
•    Valid (Store x Week) combinations can be considered as “valid opportunities to sell”
•    Valid (Store x Week) combinations in S5 is represented as a system generated metric called “strcntwk” Store Count Week.
In S5 Hindsighting, APS calculations are generated at the Store x Week x Item level.  Computations at aggregate levels (such as Class) are still performed using the store x week x item level data.  As such, Aggregate Level APS calculations in S5 Hindsighting represent true store x week x item performance. Traditionally, without S5 Hindsighting, Teams compute APS at the aggregate level using aggregate level data.
Based on the level of precision of the APS calculation being used, the resulting APS values can be very different:
This same issues we have seen  in Hindsighing  regarding APS Calculations  will persist for Target Setting.  Target setting is by definition at an Agregate level.   There should not be a difference in how the LY APS values are populated compared to how the TY APS targets are populated.

### 4.1.  APS Planning in Target Setting
S5 is  introducing a proxy for “store count week” into Target Setting.    This will provide the following:
1.    Support spreading/aggregation and correct APS calculations
2.    Better representation of APS values & logic
3.    Consistency between Hindsighting, History, and Targets for the APS calculation
Having a planned proxy for "store count week" metric will be important for target accuracy. However, this concept is not common to retail and may cause confusion to average users.   Instead, the proposal is to introduce the proxy as a more meaningful and “friendly” metric, which will be referred to as Uniformity % (aka Distortion or Dilution %)
Uniformity % will represent the difference between the Total Intersections, vs the Actual Planned Intersections. Users will have the ability to adjust the Uniformity % in order to impact the choice counts being planned.   Uniformity % will be seeded with LY values.

## The Assortment Build Phase
Once the Assortment Strategy has been finalized, users can begin the process of building the Assortment.
The Assortment Build process encompasses the activities required for developing the Assortment and the Style Colors within it. This includes Item Creation, Edits, Item Lifecycle Management, Pricing, and Ranging Strategies.
About Assortment Build
The Assortment Build process encompasses the activities required for developing a Style Color, from concept to execution. Within Assortment Build, the following will be designated for the Style Color, so the Style Color intent is clearly planned and executed to:
Where: Where will this Style Color be carried in the Assortment?
When: When will the Style Color be part of the Assortment, and what is the lifecycle of the Style Color?
Why: Why is this Style Color needed for the Assortment, what is the Style Color’s role in the Assortment, what are its key Attributes, why will the Consumer buy it?
How: How will this Style Color drive volume and contribute to the Department and Company’s goals and objectives?  (ie Price, Promotional strategies, Receipt Flows etc)
Assortment Planning Made Simple
S5 Assortment generates Plans at the Style Color, Store, Size and Week level. Providing this level of detailed planning is critical in supporting the development of Assortment Plans that are localized, accurate, and actionable. This level of planning also supports robust roll-ups and “slicing-and-dicing” of the Assortment to validate Assortment Mix and localized Product offering.

## 2.  Publish Process in S5
Now that the assortment plan has been analyzed and reconciled, it is time to “publish” to execute the buys.   Publishing is the process of sending the buy quantities to a partner system for execution.
A typical publish process is as follows:
●    User performs reconciliation, and is approved to place purchase orders.
●    Users mass-publish products and quantities at the Style,Color, SKU, Week level
●    For retailers that wish to use S5 to create master data for new products:
     ○    Style Master Data, SKU and Color information will be sent to Retailer’s host system for item creation, along with the S5 ids ( key”)
     ○    Style, Color and/or SKU master data will be created in Retailer’s host system
     ○    Once created, the retailer will send back those ids in a “client_id” field in the interface feeds, along with the original S5 key.  S5 will continue to use the same “key” for its data.
●    Order Quantities by SKU by Week will be sent to the Retailer’s Purchase Order systems
●    The retailer’s PO systems will create orders
●    Orders will be sent from the Retailer to S5 as On Order.
●    Once a Stylecolor is Published, it cannot be re-published.  Changes will need to be made in the system of record.
●    The system will provide the ability to unlock a published week. 6.3  In Season Planning Concepts

## Assortment In Season Planning Concepts
The assortment will become “in season” once actuals start to flow in (ie, actual receipts, sales, inventory etc).  In Season is defined as the period where the products have sales or inventory “actuals”.  Meaning, products have been physically received and/or have started selling to the customer.  InSeason is the time where the assortment actually comes to life, and merchants and planners can see how correct their predictions were, and how much the customer really is resonating with the assortment.
Accordingly, In Season can be a time for “reaction”, where teams can take action on products that are NOT in line with the original planned predictions (Either missing or beating their plan).
In this section we will review how S5 Assortment is used for InSeason:
1.    Types of InSeason Activities
2.    Types of InSeason Plan Changes

## 1. Types of InSeason Activities
In Season is a time for evaluation and analysis.  Were the merchant and planners' predictions accurate?  If not, how far off are they? Are they better or worse, and by how much?
Products are evaluated InSeason using the following key metrics / methodologies:
Use Hindsighting Views to evaluate FP Sell Thru
●    Teams will evaluate a product’s Full Price sell thru, to see if an item is meeting its target sales to inventory ratio.
●    Using a week level analysis is helpful, but teams also review “Life To Date” metrics, to see how the item has performed over its cumulative life.
Use Hindsighting Views to evaluate Stk/Sls
●    Teams will look at Stock to sales to understand how many “weeks on hand” (weeks of sales the current inventory level can support).
●    For example, an item with a stk to sales of 18 means, there is enough inventory to cover 18 weeks of sales (if all future weeks perform similarly to last week)
●    If an item is expected to exit in the next 4 weeks, you can clearly see that having 18 weeks’ worth of inventory is too much.
●    This will tell the teams that they must implement some kind of strategy to sell more units.
Use Hindsighting Views to evaluate Plan vs Actual Variances
●    With an assortment planning tool, teams can evaluate product actual performance vs the assortment plan.
●    This will clearly show products that are meeting, missing, and exceeding plan.
●    Products that are missing or exceeding plan will need attention from users.
Use Hindsighting Views to perform Pareto Analysis
●    Pareto analysis is intended to show users what products are contributing to the top [n]% of sales.
●    In S5, users can perform analysis to view performance by quintiles.
●    Users can also view what products are contributing to the top [n]% of sales.
●    Users can do deeper dives into these products to ensure that their inventory position can support the planned sales.

## 2. Types of InSeason Plan Changes
As stated above, if products are not meeting their plan, users have the ability to “react” and take some actions to try to change the product plan based on the revised performance expectations.
Below are the types of Changes users can execute to adjust a product’s plan inseason:
Use Assortment Build Views to Adjust On Order
●    Users can move orders up or back.
●    Users can reduce or cancel order quantities.
●    Users can increase order quantities.
●    Users can do deeper dives into these products to ensure that their inventory position can support the planned sales
Use Assortment Build Views to Make Changes to Sales Forecast
●    S5 will automatically reforecast items in season on a weekly basis based on the product’s trend.
●    Users can further reduce or increase the sales forecast, and the system will generate a revised recommended inventory plan (up or down)
Use Assortment Build Views to Make Changes to Pricing/Promo Strategy
●    Another way to influence the plan is to change the discount / promotional pricing strategy of an item.
●    Users can further reduce or increase the sales forecast, and the system will generate a revised recommended inventory plan (up or down)
●    Alternatively, for an item that is selling very well, in some cases, you may want to remove or reduce planned promotions, as they may not be needed (ie, a customer seems to be buying the product at full price… so why put a price reduction on it and unnecessarily lose profit?)
Use Assortment Build Views to Make Changes to Lifecycle
●    Another way to influence the plan is to change the discount / promotional pricing strategy of an item.
●    For example, if a product is selling poorly, it may be a good idea to mark it down earlier, in an attempt to liquidate products earlier.
●    Conversely, if a product is selling very well at Full price, users may want to extend the full price life longer and mark the product down later.
Use Assortment Build Views to Make Changes to MD Strategy
●    Users can also change how ‘aggressive’ the permanent markdown cadence is
●    This means, once a product goes to markdown, users can choose how “deep” the permanent price change is
●    For example, if a product is selling poorly, even at markdown, the user can change the MD strategy to Aggressive -- and instead of 40% the MSRP, the perm md will go to 60% or even 80% off of MSRP, in an attempt to liquidate the product sooner.
